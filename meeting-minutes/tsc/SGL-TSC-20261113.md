# 1. Introductions

The project lead opened with the state of the project. The launch is set for December 9 at Open Source Summit Japan in Tokyo, and the project is moving from the ELISA Special Interest Group to its own meetings and infrastructure. How and when that happens is for the TSC to define. From this meeting on, decisions about the project are made by the TSC as a group and no longer by the project lead alone.

Each participant introduced themselves and their work on or around Space Grade Linux.

# 2. Charter review

- **Two bodies.** The Governing Board of the Space Grade Linux Fund looks after funding, membership and marketing. The TSC sets the technical direction. The TSC Chair brings the needs of the TSC to the Governing Board.
- **Decisions.** The TSC works by consensus. A decision can be taken by email or by a vote in a meeting, as long as it is recorded for transparency.
- **Membership.** Under section 2.b every committer holds a voting seat. The charter does not say precisely what a committer is. For this first TSC it was read as everyone with a commit on the default branch of the project repository.
- **Future seats.** That reading does not scale. If the project grows as hoped, the TSC would grow with every new contributor. Other projects use a nominated group of maintainers with write access to the repository. No change was made at this meeting.
- Members who had reviewed the charter raised no objections, and its licensing terms were noted as a strength.
- The repositories were checked against section 7 of the charter: MIT for code and Creative Commons for documentation. The relicensing of the documentation is complete.

# 3. Space agency participation in the TSC

**Proposal:** give space agencies a path to a voting seat on the TSC, with each agency naming its own representative. Agency staff often cannot contribute as committers, and contractors cannot speak for an agency, so today there is no direct way for an agency to have a voice in the technical direction.

**Points raised:**

- There is no limit on the number of agencies that could ask for a seat. The TSC needs a balance between agency and commercial interests.
- Whether a seat would be automatic or would need an application and a TSC vote. The only criterion mentioned was Linux Foundation associate membership, which is free for government and nonprofit organizations.
- A preference to decide each case on the merits of the agency and of the person proposed, and not to grant a seat to any agency that asks.
- Agencies of countries that cannot work with each other. Once seated, an agency could try to keep others out, and politics could take over from the purpose of the project. Linux Foundation policy may also set limits.
- A suggestion that agency seats never make up a majority of the TSC.

**Outcome:** the TSC did not adopt a standing rule. It agreed to vote on the two agency participants present, each on their own merits and with no commitment on future seats, and to settle the permanent policy when it revisits who holds a voting seat. A written proposal that addresses the points above will follow by email.

# 4. Election of the TSC Chair

Michael Monaghan was nominated as the first TSC Chair, for his role in starting Space Grade Linux and his involvement since the beginning. The floor was opened to other candidates and nobody else came forward.

Before the vote the nominee, a civil servant, set out the limits on his role:

- He has to stay impartial and avoid conflicts of interest, so he will abstain from some votes.
- His role is strictly technical and non-fiduciary. He cannot take decisions on money, personnel or business matters.
- His participation depends on the priorities and funding of his agency.
- He cannot represent Space Grade Linux to his own agency.

**Discussion:**

- Where the Chair has to abstain, the TSC decides as a group and the executive director takes the matter to the Governing Board. A deputy chair is not needed for now.
- A secretary role was suggested so that the Chair does not also take the minutes. For now project staff take them. Other roles, such as a treasurer, can be added if the TSC is given a budget to manage.
- On balance: under the charter a motion without a majority does not pass. If the affiliations of the members ever give one organization an effective majority, the TSC should grow its membership.

# 5. RFCs for 2027

- Five RFCs are open as Draft pull requests in this repository. There was no time to discuss them, so they move to the next TSC meeting.
- Before approving them the TSC may set requirements. The example given was an identified lead for each proposed working group, to coordinate the work even if they do not write the code.
- The project lead wrote the RFCs as a starting point and asked the TSC to take over the roadmap from here.

# 6. Proposal from a member organization

- A member organization wants to contribute to the software-defined vehicle architecture work (RFC-0001), and may help lead it. Its interest is in defining the interfaces between Linux and the RTOS it uses.
- It asked for a session with the TSC the following week. The session is informational and no decision is expected. TSC members were asked to join if the time zone allows.
- It was noted that this RTOS is not under an approved open source license. Bringing it into the project would need a license exception under section 7 of the charter. Nothing of the sort is proposed at this stage.
- A TSC member reported that their organization is starting similar work with a different RTOS, with one person on it full time for about six months, and intends to keep it in sync with Space Grade Linux. The suggestion was to define the interface together, across RTOSes.
